Blown Plaster:
Repair or Replace?

19 November 2026, 09:00|Matty Anderson — NVQ Level 3 Painting & Decorating, Director
NVQ Level 3 Qualified
15+ Years Experience
CITB SkillBuild Award
Fully Insured
Free Quotes

Blown plaster isn't cracked plaster. It's plaster that has separated from the wall behind it and is now hanging there, held on by whatever is left of its edges and the paint on its face.

That distinction matters enormously, because cracked plaster can be filled and blown plaster cannot. Filler needs something to bond to. If the surface it's bonding to is itself detached from the wall, you have made a smooth version of a failing wall and bought yourself a year.

The knuckle test — diagnose it yourself in a minute

You don't need any tools for this.

Work across the wall tapping firmly with a knuckle, moving in a grid pattern. Listen to the difference between one area and another.

  • Solid, dull, dead sound — the plaster is bonded to the masonry. Whatever is wrong with it is a surface problem and it fills.
  • Hollow, drummy, resonant sound — there is a void behind it. The plaster has blown.

Once you have heard both on the same wall you will never mistake one for the other again.

Map the hollow area by tapping outwards from the centre until the sound goes solid. That boundary is roughly where the repair will need to cut back to, and it is almost always considerably larger than the visible damage suggests.

Why it happens

Damp. Far and away the most common cause. Moisture moving through the wall breaks down the bond between the backing coat and the masonry.

Salt. Specific to coastal properties — very common across Southport, Birkdale, Ainsdale and Formby. Salt carried in moisture crystallises within the plaster, expands, and physically pushes it off the wall. Salt is hygroscopic, meaning it draws moisture out of the air indefinitely, so it keeps working even after the original water ingress is fixed.

Poor original key. Plaster needs a mechanical grip on the substrate. Smooth engineering brick, over-trowelled render, or a dusty surface at the time the backing coat went on can all mean the bond was weak from day one.

Age. Lime plaster on a solid wall in a Victorian terrace in Burnley or Nelson has often been in place for 120 years. Nothing lasts forever.

Impact. A localised knock — a door handle hitting the wall repeatedly, furniture moved against it — starts a small blown patch that spreads outward from there.

Skimming over an earlier problem. The self-inflicted one, and we see it regularly. Someone skimmed over blown plaster to make a property presentable for a sale or a letting. Now there are two detached layers instead of one.

Repair or replace?

Patch repair is right when the blown area is contained, the wall around it genuinely sounds solid, and the cause has been dealt with. Cut back to sound plaster, apply the appropriate backing coat, finish to match.

Full wall re-plaster is right when more than roughly a third of the wall sounds hollow, when there are several separate blown patches, or when the plaster is old lime that is generally at the end of its life.

The honest test: if you're tapping and finding a third or more of the wall drummy, patching is a false economy. You will be back, and each patch is a visible repair.

What a proper repair involves

1. Deal with the cause first. Non-negotiable. If moisture is still coming in, no plaster repair will hold. That might mean repointing, a gutter, a cill, a leak, or a damp-proof course. Plastering before that is paying to postpone the problem.

2. Hack off back to sound. All blown plaster removed, back to bare masonry, cut back to where tapping sounds solid. Dusty work, which is why the room needs proper protection and the doorways sealing.

3. Let the wall dry. If damp was involved, the masonry itself is wet. Plastering onto wet brick restarts the cycle. This can mean weeks, and it is the stage most likely to be rushed by someone working to a deadline.

4. Treat if needed. Salt-contaminated walls need a salt-retardant render system or a membrane. Standard backing coat over salt-laden brick will blow again.

5. Backing coat. Bonding on smooth or low-suction surfaces, browning or hardwall on standard brick. Applied and scratched to key, then left to set properly before anything goes over it.

6. Finish coat. Two-coat skim, trowelled up to match the surrounding surface.

7. Drying. Seven to fourteen days for a skim, up to a month where a full backing coat has gone on. Then a mist coat before any full-strength paint — drying times before painting covers why that matters.

Why skimming over it never works

Worth being blunt, because it gets offered often — usually by someone who wants a quick job and a quick invoice.

A skim is 2 to 3mm of finish plaster bonded to whatever is underneath it. If that surface is already detached from the wall, all you have done is add weight to something that is already falling off.

It looks perfect for a year, sometimes two. Then it cracks along the edge of the blown area, and the repair now involves removing two layers rather than one.

If you've had a wall skimmed and it has blown again in the same place, that is exactly what happened.

When it isn't blown plaster

Two things get confused with it regularly:

Efflorescence — a white, powdery crystalline deposit on the surface. That's salts coming out as the wall dries, and the plaster underneath may be perfectly sound. Brush it off dry, never wash it, and let the wall finish drying.

Cracking with no hollowness — if it taps solid, it is a crack rather than a blowout, and the repair is completely different. The six causes of cracked plaster sets out which is which and which ones matter.

Frequently asked questions

What does blown plaster sound like?

Hollow and drummy, like tapping a cardboard box, compared with the dull dead sound of plaster still bonded to the wall. The difference is obvious once you've heard both on the same wall.

Can you just fill blown plaster?

No. Filler bonds to the surface it's applied to. If that surface is detached from the wall, the repair is detached too. It has to be cut out and replaced.

How much does it cost to replaster one wall?

£380 to £650 for an average bedroom wall, hacked off and re-plastered. Per square metre that's £45 to £75, rising to £65–£95 where a salt-retardant system is needed on coastal property.

Does blown plaster always mean damp?

Not always — age, poor original key and impact damage all cause it. But if it's low on an external wall or around a chimney breast, assume damp until something proves otherwise.

How long does a blown plaster repair take to dry?

A patch skim, seven to fourteen days. A full hack-off with backing coat, two to four weeks. If the masonry behind was wet, add the drying time for the wall itself before any plaster goes on — that can be several weeks more again.

Can I paint over blown plaster as a temporary fix?

You can, and it will look acceptable briefly. Paint adds no strength, the plaster carries on detaching, and you'll pay to remove both layers later. It's a fix that costs more than doing nothing at all.

Is blown plaster a structural problem?

Usually not in itself — it's a surface failure. But the damp that caused it can be, and persistent moisture in a wall will eventually affect timbers and mortar. The plaster is the symptom worth paying attention to.
