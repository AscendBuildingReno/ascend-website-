Decorating prices in Burnley sit meaningfully below the national average, and well below anything you'll see quoted on a London-weighted price comparison site. A standard double bedroom, walls and ceiling, painted properly with two coats over prepared surfaces, is £280 to £450 in 2026. A three-bedroom terrace done throughout is typically £2,200 to £3,800.

What follows is the detail behind those numbers, and the things about Burnley's housing stock that genuinely change what a job costs.

Ranges are wide because condition drives price more than size does. A room that's been maintained and needs a wash down and two coats is at the bottom of the range. The same room with woodchip to strip, filling throughout and a dark colour to cover is at the top.

What Burnley's housing stock does to the price

This isn't the same job everywhere in the country, and there are three things about local properties that change the numbers.

Stone terraces and solid walls

Large parts of Burnley, Padiham, Brierfield and Harle Syke are pre-1919 stone or brick terraces with solid walls and no cavity. Two consequences follow. First, external walls run colder, which means condensation and mould turn up in corners and behind furniture far more often than in cavity-wall properties — and that needs treating properly rather than painting over. Second, the plaster is often original and has usually been patched several times, so surfaces need more preparation before they'll take a flat finish.

High ceilings in the older stock

A lot of Victorian and Edwardian terraces round here have ceilings at 2.7m to 2.9m rather than the modern 2.4m. That's roughly 15–20% more wall area per room, plus the extra time of working off a hop-up or tower. It's a real cost, and any quote that ignores it is a quote that's going to change halfway through.

Original features worth keeping

Picture rails, deep skirtings, panelled doors and original cornice are common and worth preserving. They also take considerably longer to paint properly than a flush modern door and a 100mm skirting. A panelled door done properly — cleaned, filled, sanded, primed where bare, two coats — is 45 minutes to an hour. There are usually eight or nine of them in a three-bed terrace.

What should be in the price

A quote that doesn't mention preparation is a quote for painting over problems. Ours specifies all of the following, and yours should too:

  • Protection — dust sheets on all floors, furniture covered or moved and covered, sockets and switches masked
  • Washing down — sugar soap on all surfaces being painted, particularly kitchens and above radiators
  • Filling and making good — cracks raked out and filled, nail holes filled, gaps caulked between woodwork and wall
  • Sanding — filler sanded flush, gloss surfaces keyed before repainting
  • Priming — bare wood, bare plaster, filler patches and any stains blocked
  • Two full coats as standard on every surface, not "one coat if it covers"
  • Number of coats stated per surface and the product being used
  • Clean-up and waste removal at the end of the job

What tends to add cost

Woodchip or heavy relief paper. Stripping it is slow, wet and messy, and what's underneath is frequently poor. Budget £120–£220 per room for stripping, plus whatever the plaster underneath needs.

Dark to light colour changes. Going from a deep colour to white or magnolia is three coats, sometimes four, or two coats over a tinted primer. Either way, more than two.

Damp or mould. It has to be treated at source before decorating, or it's back within a year. Painting over black mould with standard emulsion is a job you'll be paying for twice.

Ceilings with nicotine or water staining. Needs a stain-blocking primer, otherwise it bleeds through emulsion no matter how many coats go on.

Artex. Either skimmed over or overboarded. Anything pre-1985 should be tested for asbestos before it's disturbed — that's not optional and it's not scaremongering.

These are Burnley and East Lancashire figures. The same schedule of work in London or the South East is routinely 30–50% more, which is why national estimating tools read as wrong when you're getting quotes locally.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a painter and decorator charge per day in Burnley?

Between £180 and £250 a day for a time-served decorator in 2026. Most domestic work is better quoted as a fixed price for the job rather than a day rate, because it removes the risk of the job taking longer than expected from you and puts it on the contractor.

How long does it take to decorate a three-bedroom house?

Typically five to nine working days for one decorator, depending on condition and how much woodwork is involved. Stripping wallpaper, heavy filling or colour changes from dark to light can add two or three days.

Do I need to move out while decorating is done?

No. Most jobs are done room by room so you keep the rest of the house usable. It's worth clearing one room at a time ahead of us arriving, and children and pets are better kept out of the room being worked in.

Do you supply the paint or should I?

Either. We can supply at trade prices, which usually works out similar to retail after the discount, or you can buy your own if you've a specific colour in mind. If you supply it, buy slightly more than the calculator suggests — running out mid-wall on a colour that then can't be matched exactly is a genuine problem.

Are you insured?

Yes. Ascend Building Renovations Ltd holds public liability insurance and we're happy to provide a copy of the certificate before work starts. Any contractor who won't is one to walk away from.

Do you do plastering as well as decorating?

Yes. Plastering and decorating in one visit avoids the gap where you're waiting for a second trade to become available, and it means one contractor is responsible for the finish rather than two blaming each other.
