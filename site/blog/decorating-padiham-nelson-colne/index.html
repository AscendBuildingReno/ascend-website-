What Pendle's housing stock changes

Solid stone walls, and no cavity

The mill-town terraces through Nelson, Colne, Brierfield and Padiham are overwhelmingly pre-1919 stone or brick with solid walls. There's no cavity, and in most cases no insulation.

That has a consequence people live with for years without understanding. External walls run cold. Warm, moist air from cooking, showering and drying washing hits that cold surface and condenses on it. You get black spot mould in bedroom corners, above skirtings, behind wardrobes and in the top corners of north-facing rooms.

This is the single most common thing we get called about in these towns, and it is not a decorating problem. Painting over it with standard emulsion does nothing — it's back by spring. It has to be treated at source, the moisture and ventilation addressed, and then decorated with a product suited to the wall.

Anyone who quotes you for "painting over the damp patch" without asking why it's there is not going to solve it.

Blown plaster on external walls

Where solid walls have carried moisture for decades, the plaster loses its bond with the masonry behind. The tell is simple — tap the wall with a knuckle. Solid, dull sound means it's bonded. Hollow and drummy means it's blown.

It concentrates in the first 600mm above skirting level on external walls, and around chimney breasts. Skimming over it buys you eighteen months before it cracks along the edge, and then there are two layers to remove instead of one. How to spot blown plaster covers the test and the repair properly.

Chimney breasts

Nearly every terrace round here has them, often two or three, frequently with the fireplace removed and the opening blocked up. Two recurring problems:

Staining that bleeds through. Soot and salts in the old flue migrate through the plaster and come through emulsion no matter how many coats go on. It needs a stain-blocking primer, not more paint.

Damp from a capped stack. A flue capped without ventilation traps moisture, which then comes through the breast. Fixing the decoration without fixing the cap is money spent twice.

High ceilings in the older stock

A lot of the Victorian and Edwardian terraces here sit at 2.7m to 2.9m rather than the modern 2.4m. That's 15 to 20% more wall area per room, plus the time of working off a hop-up or tower rather than off the floor. A quote priced from floor area will come up short and the shortfall gets found halfway through the job.

Original features worth keeping

Picture rails, deep skirtings, panelled doors and cast iron fireplaces are common across Pendle and worth preserving. They also take considerably longer to paint properly than modern trim. A panelled door done correctly — cleaned, filled, sanded, primed where bare, two coats — is close to an hour. A three-bed terrace usually has eight or nine of them.

Weather and exterior cycles

Pendle sits higher and wetter than most of Lancashire. West-facing elevations take the weather hard, and exterior coatings here typically need attention every six to eight years rather than the eight to ten you'd expect further south.

There's also a specific issue with solid stone: it needs to breathe. A plastic-heavy masonry paint traps moisture inside the wall, and within a few years it blisters off, sometimes taking the face of the stone with it. Breathable mineral paint costs more per tin and is the correct answer on this stock. It's the difference between a decorating spend and a fabric repair.

Plastering and decorating in one visit

We do both trades, which matters more than it sounds on this housing stock. A decorating job here frequently becomes a plastering job the moment the wallpaper comes off — woodchip that's been up since the seventies is very often hiding plaster that needs skimming, because that's precisely what it was put up to hide.

One contractor for both removes the gap where you're waiting weeks for a plasterer to become available, and it means one person is accountable for the final finish rather than two trades pointing at each other.

Frequently asked questions

Do you charge extra to come out to Nelson or Colne?

No. Pendle is part of our core working area, not an outlying one. Quotes are the same as they'd be in Burnley itself.

How much does a decorator charge per day in Pendle?

£180 to £240 a day in 2026. Most domestic work is better quoted as a fixed price for the job rather than a day rate — it moves the risk of the job overrunning onto the contractor instead of you.

Why does my terraced house keep getting mould in the corners?

Solid walls with no cavity run cold, and warm moist air condenses on cold surfaces. It's a ventilation and insulation issue rather than a paint issue. It needs treating at source and then decorating with a suitable product. Painting over it alone will not hold past the next winter.

Can you strip woodchip wallpaper?

Yes, budget £120 to £220 per room. Be prepared for the plaster underneath to need attention — woodchip was frequently used to hide poor surfaces, and often that's exactly what it's been doing.

Do you do exterior work on stone terraces?

Yes, and we'd specify a breathable mineral paint rather than a standard plastic masonry paint. Solid stone needs to release moisture. Sealing it in causes blistering and can damage the stone face itself.

How far in advance do I need to book?

Two to four weeks for interior work at most times of year. Exterior work in spring and summer books further ahead — six to eight weeks is common for a May or June slot.
