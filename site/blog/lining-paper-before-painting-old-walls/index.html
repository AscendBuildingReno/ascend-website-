Do Old Walls Need
Lining Paper Before Painting?

Lining paper is plain, heavy paper you hang on a wall before painting it. On the older terraces round Burnley, Padiham, Nelson and Colne it's often the difference between a wall that looks smooth and one that shows every old repair as soon as the light hits it from the side.

It isn't the answer for every wall, though. On some walls it just hides a problem for a year, and then you're paying to strip it off again. This is how we decide.

What lining paper actually does

Old plaster has usually been patched many times over the years, with different fillers and different plasters. Each patch takes paint differently, so you get dull and shiny areas that show through coat after coat. Lining paper gives you one even surface that soaks up paint the same all over.

It also bridges fine hairline cracks, crazing and small dents that filling alone won't fully hide.

What it doesn't do is fix anything. It won't stop a wall moving, it won't stop damp, and it won't hold loose plaster on. It covers the surface, and that's all.

Signs your walls would suit it

  • Crazing. Lots of fine, spidery cracks across the plaster. You see this a lot on the old lime plaster in pre-1919 terraces.
  • Patchwork walls. Years of filled holes, old plug holes and mixed repairs that still show after two coats of paint.
  • Patches that dry a different sheen every time the room is painted.
  • A sound but rough surface after stripping woodchip or old wallpaper, where the plaster is still firm but marked.
  • Rooms where a full re-skim is too much, such as a rental between tenants, as long as the plaster underneath is sound.

When lining paper is the wrong fix

Blown plaster

Tap the wall with a knuckle. A solid, dull sound means the plaster is bonded. A hollow, drummy sound means it has come away from the brick or stone behind. Paper over that and it'll crack and bulge again. The loose plaster needs cutting out and re-plastering first. Our post on why plaster cracks goes through the causes.

Damp and mould

Paper and paste hold moisture, and mould will grow behind them. If a wall is damp, or keeps getting black mould in the corners, find out why and sort that first. See our page on damp and mould-affected walls.

Cracks that keep opening

If a crack comes back in the same place every time it's filled, the wall is still moving. The paper will just split along the same line.

Bathrooms and wet areas

Steam and splashes can lift the joins. Behind a basin or near a shower, a properly prepared plaster wall with a suitable paint, or tiles, is the better choice.

Badly uneven walls

Lining paper follows the wall. It doesn't flatten it. Humps, dips and bowed sections will still show, especially under a ceiling light. That's a skim job.

Lining paper or a skim coat?

A skim gives you a new flat surface. It's the right answer where the plaster is tired, uneven or patched beyond saving. It does need time to dry before you can decorate, usually days rather than hours. Our guide on how long new plaster takes to dry covers that. Have a look at our skimming services in Burnley too.

Lining paper is quicker and less messy, and you can usually paint the next day. It only works if the plaster behind it is sound.

Plenty of jobs use both. On a Burnley living room in our portfolio, we stripped the walls back, repaired and re-skimmed where it was needed, then lined the walls before decorating. We've also hung lining paper in a Burnley bedroom as part of a home refresh.

How lining paper is hung for a painted finish

  1. Strip any old wallpaper. Don't line over it. If the old paper lets go later, the lining paper comes with it.
  2. Fill and sand. Lining paper hides fine marks, not holes or ridges. Rake out and fill the cracks, then sand everything flush.
  3. Size the wall. Bare or porous plaster gets a coat of diluted paste first. That stops the wall sucking the paste dry and lets the paper slide into place.
  4. Pick the grade. Lining paper is sold by grade, from around 800 up to 2000. The higher the number, the thicker the paper. Thicker paper covers more, but it's heavier and harder to get tight into corners. A mid grade such as 1200 or 1400 is a common choice for painting over.
  5. Paste and soak evenly. Follow the paste and paper instructions, and give every length the same soaking time so they all stretch the same.
  6. Butt the joins. Push each edge tight up to the last one. Never overlap, because an overlap shows as a ridge under paint. For painting, it's normally hung upright like wallpaper. Hanging it sideways, called cross-lining, is mainly used under wallpaper so the joins don't line up.
  7. Trim and caulk. Trim neatly at the ceiling and skirting. Run a thin bead of decorators' caulk into internal corners and any join that has opened slightly.
  8. Let it dry. At least overnight before any paint goes on.
  9. Paint. First coat slightly thinned, as the tin allows, then full coats. Small bubbles often flatten out as the paint dries. Any that don't can be slit and pasted down.

Which paint finish to use

Matt. A matt emulsion hides joins and small bumps far better than anything with a sheen. Silk and other shiny finishes pick out every join as soon as light falls across the wall. Our guide to matt, eggshell, satin and gloss explains where each finish belongs.

For landlords

On a sound wall that's just tired and patched, lining paper and a hard-wearing matt can tidy a room between tenants without the drying time of a re-skim. If there's damp, mould or loose plaster, that comes first, otherwise the problem is back before the next tenancy ends. See our landlord decorating and property refresh page.

Frequently asked questions

Can you paint straight onto lining paper?

Yes, once it's fully dry. Leave it at least overnight. Put the first coat on slightly thinned, as the tin allows, then follow with full coats of matt emulsion.

Will lining paper hide cracks in my walls?

It hides fine hairline cracks and crazing well. It won't hold back a crack that keeps opening, because the wall is still moving underneath and the paper splits along the same line.

Is lining paper cheaper than having the walls skimmed?

It depends on the wall. On sound but tired plaster, lining is usually quicker, and you can paint the next day. On loose, blown or very uneven plaster it isn't a real option, so the comparison doesn't apply. We'll tell you which one your walls need when we price it.

Can lining paper go on a ceiling?

Yes, on a ceiling that's cracked but sound. If the ceiling sags, bounces when you press it or sounds hollow, don't paper it. It needs repairing or boarding first. See our ceilings and plasterboard page.
